A connected vehicle on a private LTE network, and what it would take to run it on a motorway.

Four machines: a 4G core, an eNodeB on software radio, a car as user equipment, and an inference node. The car captures and obeys; the core decides. Then the same service planned for the A-601 between Valladolid and Segovia: traffic, radio coverage, cost and licensing.

Network

srsRAN 4G end to end. The EPC (MME, HSS, SPGW) runs on one machine and hands UEs addresses in 172.16.0.0/24; the eNodeB runs srsenb on a bladeRF on a second machine and does nothing but radio. The car attaches through an LTE modem; the Jetson is reached from the core over HTTP. No database and no monolith in the critical path: UDP between car and core, HTTP between core and inference.

Vision

A traffic-sign detector trained on a dataset built for the project: 4,985 images and 10,962 annotations of the signs on the test track, 300 epochs on a YOLO backbone. Served behind a Roboflow-compatible endpoint on the Jetson, with a local fallback on the EPC. Every session is recorded as annotated video plus a frame manifest, so hard cases are relabelled and the model retrained without touching the control path.

Control

Everything that decides lives in the core. coche.py receives frames, battery, IMU and LiDAR over UDP and answers with a compact steering and throttle packet. The command is built in layers; each one can tighten the previous, none can loosen safety.

State machine. Detections become actions deterministically: a confirmed sign over an area and persistence threshold triggers a 90° turn with a manoeuvre lock; speed-limit 30 and 90 set throttle 0.50 and 0.70 for three seconds; STOP and no-entry hold neutral for five; stale frames or predictions fall back to neutral.
Lane assistance. OpenCV detection of the blue and green tapes on the track, applying a bounded steering correction only on forward autonomous actions.
Speed control. BMI160 IMU telemetry gives a bounded forward-speed estimate; a PID on the throttle holds the target when the track changes friction, never overriding LiDAR or the camera decision.
LiDAR safety. A 45-beam front sector: an obstacle under 0.15 m forces a stop, 0.15–0.85 m caps throttle, 0.85–1.35 m adds steering correction. LiDAR vetoes the camera; a stale LiDAR does not.
Operator console. Annotated live camera, manual and autonomous modes, hot tuning of cruise throttle, steering trim, lane gain and LiDAR sectors, telemetry, recording. 8088/TCP on the EPC.
Watchdog. If the core stops sending commands past the timeout, the car slows down on its own. Radio loss degrades safely.

Demonstration

Three minutes on the lab track: the car enters autonomous mode, drives a sequence of signs built for the prototype, stops for a box placed in its way, and hands control back to the operator from the console.

  1. Autonomous mode. The operator switches modes from the console; lane assistance stabilises the car on the tapes.
  2. Left turn. The state machine executes a 90° turn once the detection clears area and persistence; trim is suspended to keep the lock.
  3. Speed 90. Throttle ramps to 0.70 for three seconds, then back to the cruise set on the console.
  4. STOP. Neutral for five seconds.
  5. Right turn. Same policy, other side, after the cooldown.
  6. Speed 30. Throttle 0.50 for three seconds; with fresh IMU data the PID holds the target.
  7. Obstacle. A box in front of the moving car: lidar_processor.py forces a stop under 0.15 m.
  8. Back to manual. The operator takes steering and throttle; the UDP source changes, nothing restarts.

Taking it to the A-601

The second half of the work plans the same service on the Valladolid–Segovia motorway, km 0 to 104, on 10 MHz of band 7 ceded by Vodafone under an academic contract. Traffic sizes the capacity, the radio study sizes the sites, and the cost and licensing chapters say what it takes to switch it on.

Radio coverage

Multi-transmitter study in Xirio-Online, 19 May 2026: COST-231 Hata with a 72 % rural correction fitted to the corridor, 25 masts (20 co-located on existing sites, 5 new), dual sectorisation, 44 of 50 sectors effective over the carriageway, 16-QAM as the dimensioning modulation. Percentages are over the served area; the study rectangle includes a wide strip on each side of the road that is not a target.

Cost and licensing

Cost. CAPEX for civil works, radio equipment, engineering and permits; recurring OPEX for co-location, power and maintenance; the first-year total. Figures and assumptions are in the economic chapter of the report.
Licensing. Ley 11/2022 General de Telecomunicaciones; RD 123/2017 on use of the radio spectrum; RD 1066/2001 on exposure limits; procedure before SETID and registration with the CNMC.
